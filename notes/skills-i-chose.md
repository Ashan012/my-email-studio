Use these: the two sources you recommend for writing campaigns and sending email, and why.

- coreyhaines31/marketingskills (owner: Corey Haines) — Best for writing campaigns/sequences. It has skills like `copywriting` (237.4K installs), `cold-email` (128.9K installs), `emails` (79.4K installs), etc. Lots of marketing-specific guidance (CRO/copywriting/strategy) that maps well to building campaigns and sequences from scratch. Very popular on skills.sh.
- resend/resend-skills (owner: Resend, official) — Best for sending email. The official Resend skills (`resend` 23.7K installs, `agent-email-inbox` 13.5K installs, `resend-cli` 13.5K installs) are published by Resend itself and cover sending/receiving/inbox/CLI. Also `resend/email-best-practices` (8.1K installs) and `resend/react-email` (9.8K installs) are official. If you're actually sending via Resend, these are the most direct/trusted sources.

Avoid these: two results you would not trust, and why.

- open.feishu.cn@lark-mail / larksuite/cli@lark-mail (Feishu/Lark) — High install counts (816.5K / 469.2K) but this is for Lark Mail (Feishu ecosystem), not general email marketing/campaign sending. If you're not using Feishu/Lark, it's irrelevant and may pull in unrelated workflows.
- mbfinotti/advertising-skills (e.g. `ad-budget-pacing` 21.7K, `thought-leadership-ads` 21.1K) — More about paid ads/budget pacing than writing marketing emails or sending them. Not a good fit for an "email marketing studio" focus; could be misleading if you install expecting email campaign help.

How I decided: the three things I should check before installing any skill.

1. Publisher/reputation — Prefer official sources (like `resend` for Resend) or well-known builders. Check the GitHub owner/org (is it the product vendor or a reputable author?).
2. Fit for the job — Does it actually solve your task (campaign writing vs ad buying vs a specific mail client)? High installs don't mean relevant to email marketing.
3. Signals of quality/popularity — Look at installs on skills.sh and stars/activity on the GitHub repo. Be cautious with very high installs from tools you don't use, or low-signal repos with little context.
